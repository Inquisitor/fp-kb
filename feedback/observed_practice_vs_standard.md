---
name: Observed practice is an argument, not the standard
description: A survey answers "how is it now" - one argument among several. Poor discipline is not a reason to write itself into the rule, and a divergence between practice and the stated standard is a question to raise, not to settle silently
type: feedback
---

Where no standard has been set, deriving one from what is already done is legitimate. It answers a
useful question: **how is it now.**

That is one argument, not a verdict. It can be adopted or rejected on the merits — purpose, cost,
common sense.

**Poor discipline is not a reason to write it into the rule.** A field almost nobody fills is not
thereby a field that should stay empty; a step almost everybody skips is not thereby optional. The
count has found a gap, and closing gaps is what a standard is for. Write the observed rate into the
standard and the gap becomes permanent: until then the behaviour was a lapse, correctable by
pointing at the rule; afterwards it *is* the rule.

**When practice contradicts the stated standard, raise it.** Same move as active criticism, and the
same move as when something contradicts what the user said: the divergence is a question, not a
mandate in either direction. Do not silently promote the practice to a rule, and do not silently
suppress it either — surface it and decide.
