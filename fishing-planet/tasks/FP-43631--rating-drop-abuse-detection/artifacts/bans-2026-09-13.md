## Rule 3 corrected -- count events, not games

The old counter measured the wrong quantity. The offence consists of *not* playing, so the harder a
candidate drains the fewer games he has, and the more readily the leniency shelters him: at a 68%
unproductive share it takes more than 30 registrations to reach 10 games.

This cycle **3 of 12 candidates fell under the old counter** -- FOGGIA1920 at 8 games, Lesky2123 at
8, ZellyRolled at 9 -- and it was named decisive in 2 of the resulting releases, ZellyRolled and
FOGGIA1920. For Lesky2123 the court recorded rule 3 as available but did not rest on it; his case is
independently weak, shedding 91 rating against a screening line of 90 and holding 36 lifetime prizes
against 4 in the window.

The threshold cannot simply be restated in events at 10, because the screen already requires 6
unproductive events and more than 3 prizes and a prize requires a start, so **every candidate
reaching review has at least 10 events by construction**. 10 is the floor, not a measure. 15 sits
above it and reaches the cases the rule is for: at 11 events a candidate is 6 unproductive and 5
played with 4 prizes -- a stark ratio on denominators far too small to carry it.

Against this cohort, a 15-event threshold would shelter only Lesky2123 at 14 registrations, and
ZellyRolled at 28 would not come close. Note what that means honestly: for Lesky2123 the new rule
would *create* a shelter the old counter did not give him, since his release did not rest on rule 3.
The rewrite is not uniformly stricter -- it moves the leniency onto a defensible measure, and on this
cohort that happens to tighten it for the case that mattered and loosen it for the weakest one.

Not applied retroactively: this cycle's verdicts stand on the rules in force when they were heard.

## The week boundary -- a correction to the risk-zone procedure

Raised by the operator and verified. A competition that starts inside the week but **ends exactly on
the boundary counts to the following week**. Competition 331553 ran 2026-09-06 22:00 -> 09-07 00:00;
its 2 winners do not appear in the closing week's history at all and carry those wins in the next
period.

Consequences:
- **The last competition counting to a week is the one ending at 22:00 UTC**, i.e. starting at 20:00.
  From 22:00 UTC on Sunday the weekly board is final.
- The drift allowance added earlier the same day -- bounding a candidate's remaining climb by the
  competitions he is still registered for -- is unnecessary when the zone is computed after 22:00,
  and it produced a wrong answer here: Pilou62 was credited with a possible extra win from a
  competition that cannot count to this week.
- **The screen and the leaderboard use different boundaries.** The screen filters by `StartDate`, the
  board attributes by end. A competition starting Sunday 22:00 therefore falls inside our sweep and
  inside the *next* week's board. It is a **pair** each week, not one: the Sunday 22:00 competition
  enters our screen without reaching the closing board, and the *previous* Sunday's 22:00 competition
  reaches the board without having entered the screen. Competition 331553 is the second kind.
- **The boundary competition is charged in no sweep week at all**, which is worse than a reporting
  mismatch. The screen takes `StartDate >= @WindowStart AND IsEnded = 1`. A competition starting
  Sunday 22:00 has not ended when that week's sweep runs, so it is excluded then; the following
  week's sweep starts after its `StartDate`, so it is excluded again. It falls through both. This is
  a recurring weekly blind spot in detection and it is not fixed by anything in this cycle.
- The screen's window has **no upper bound**, so its effective span depends on when the script is
  run. That is the mechanism behind the contaminated post-ban re-run recorded in week-18.
