# Week-18 ban record -- 2026-09-06

First cycle on the drain-inclusive screen, and the first cycle where the review had to be run twice
because the brief it was given was wrong.

## The screen change, and what it caught

From this cycle the detection SQL selects on **unproductive participation** -- no-shows plus
zero-score finishes -- rather than absence alone. Cohort 17 against 13 under the old criteria.

**Myky0576 is the vindication and the whole argument for the change.** 42 registrations, 41 starts,
**1** no-show, 27 finishes that scored nothing. His absence share is 2.38%, so the old screen could
never have seen him at any threshold worth running. He also turned out to be a repeat offender on
our own ban from week-2, and to have been under a competition ban until 2026-09-01 that he re-entered
the same pattern the day it expired.

3 other convictions came from the wider screen: Ilija1982, codeco and ST-9257 all carry
zero-score entries, though only ST-9257 in volume (11 of his 19 unproductive entries).

## The briefing defect -- the important finding of this cycle

The first hearing returned **4 BAN of 17**, against 11 of 16 the cycle before. The cohort had not got
softer. The brief had.

The pre-trial brief omitted the standing week-12 finding that **a same-second ledger group is a flush
moment, not evidence of presence**. Prosecutors therefore built their rule 1(a) sequence cases on
exactly that artifact, and judges -- correctly, and by going to the raw log -- struck them down. The
clearest statement of it is in the o Lord Mac o first-hearing verdict: the prosecution's marquee fact
was a no-show booking at the same second a prior competition resolved, and the judge showed the
player had been scoring inside that prior competition for nearly 4 hours, so no decision could
have been read from the ordering at all.

A second, smaller omission compounded it: the brief did not carry the operator's week-12 rejection of
the boundary-targeting argument, so the seagate22022 judge was free to reason that an absence cadence
unchanged from PCR 44 to 174 showed the absences were untargeted -- a line already considered and
rejected on the grounds that a player winning inside the bottom bracket must shed continuously to
stay there.

The 13 unconvicted candidates were re-heard on a corrected brief which restored both findings and,
additionally, stated what rule 1(a)'s temporal limb can be proved from at all: the rating chain of
resolved competitions, never the ordering inside one second. **5 of 13 flipped to BAN**, and 1
EXONERATE (Novan07) tightened to WATCH.

**This is a process failure, not a rules failure.** Rule 1 was not changed and should not be. The
correction belongs in the pre-trial checklist.

seagate22022 and o Lord Mac o, both left on WATCH by the first hearing, both unbanned, with the

## 2 checks run for the first time

**Outage test (Steam).** The defence is entitled to argue that absences are a platform or connection
incident rather than a choice, and the argument had never been tested. Measured over the sweep week:
the platform-wide no-show rate is **38.42%** across 82 competitions; the 99 candidate no-shows fall
in competitions averaging 41.06% (range 19-66%), and **none** in a competition at twice the baseline.
No clustering, so the outage reading is not available on the evidence. PS and Xbox were not measured.

This also produced an unplanned finding worth acting on: **the screen's 30% unproductive-share
threshold sits below the platform's own baseline no-show rate of 38.42%.** As a filter it currently
selects nothing on its own; the selectivity comes entirely from the other 3 criteria. That
belongs with the standing backlog item on thresholds.

**Fish-release check.** Releasing caught fish is the one mechanism that reduces a result on purpose,
so releases landing in a zero-score competition would be directly aggravating. Measured across the
cohort: releases exist and are sometimes heavy (ST-9257 34 across 12 competitions, anatoly2026 15
across 5, Leelos90 7, codeco 2) but the intersection with zero-score competitions is **empty**.
People release where they go on to score, which is the legitimate keepnet-management case. Worth
keeping as a standing pre-trial check precisely because a hit would be strong evidence.

## Evidence defects found while building the cards

- **The ledger under-reports.** Competitions carrying a process marker with no reward line are
  systemic, not rare -- 21 of 38 for jujiito, 10 for ST-9257. A count taken from the ledger is a
  floor, and absence of a ledger entry is not evidence that nothing happened.
- **Printed deltas overstate the loss where rating floors at zero** -- 12 entries across 5
  candidates. Known behaviour, but the card's summed fields use the printed values.
- **jujiito flagged `evidence_completeness: degraded`** (ledger 3 no-shows in the charge sub-range
  against SQL's 10) and was held on WATCH.

All 3 were written into the corrected brief as named defects binding on both sides.
