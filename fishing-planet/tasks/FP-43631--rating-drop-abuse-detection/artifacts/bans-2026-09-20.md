## Two worked examples of ordinary play — keep these

The repertoire the brief carries has always described abuse. It carries nothing showing what
innocent heavy absence looks like, and that is half the reason the same arguments get re-litigated
every cycle. These two are the missing half.

**`MP_Alan` — the engaged player on a schedule.** 109 registrations in 14 days, 63 played, often
consecutive 2-hour slots. Results are a run of small losses among occasional large gains, the
profile of a real player in a strong field. 13 of his 16 slots between 20:00 and 02:00 are missed
against 8 of 43 elsewhere; the no-show rate *falls* as registration rating rises (60% under 900,
33% at 1000+), which is the opposite of rating management and settles it.

**`EZ-Enlightened1` — the player who does not care about rating.** He registers runs of
consecutive slots and attends whichever he happens to be at the console for, with no pattern
inside a batch:

```
09-12 17:12  registered 3:  20:00 missed · 22:00 missed · 00:00 PLAYED, 16th
09-12 05:55  registered 4:  08:00 missed · 10:00 missed · 14:00 played · 16:00 played
09-13 00:47  registered 3:  04:00 missed · 06:00 missed · 08:00 missed
09-13 13:04  registered 2:  14:00 missed · 16:00 missed
09-13 14:16  registered 2:  20:00 missed · 22:00 missed
```

He never unregisters — cancelling forecloses the chance of dropping in, and the penalty costs him
nothing that matters. Across 13 September his rating drifted 2342 → 2222, 120 points in a day, and
it changed nothing: he stayed a thousand points clear of any boundary.

**`xFenrir77` — the player who plays by mood.** Registration and attendance are decided separately
and neither follows a plan:

```
09-10 07:26  registered 4:  08:00 missed · 12:00 played · 16:00 played · 18:00 played
09-10 13:50  registered 1 slot ten minutes before its 14:00 start — and never turned up,
             though a competition runs two hours and the qualifying half-hour could be
             fished at any point inside it
09-11 11:38  registered 4:  12:00 played · 14:00 played (3rd, +45) · 16:00 played · 18:00 missed
09-12        06:00 registered at 01:35 · 08:00 registered the previous evening at 20:17 ·
             10:00 at 01:35 · 20:00 at 11:59 — a day played off a mix of same-day and
             overnight registrations, taking 1st, 1st and 2nd
```

He registers when the mood takes him and attends when it takes him, sometimes ten minutes after
signing up and sometimes not at all. There is no arrangement here to find.

**That contrast is the point.** The penalty is identical at every rating and means something
completely different. `EZ-Enlightened1` loses 120 in a day and it is noise. `CrazyGepard` loses 73
in a day and changes bracket. A detector that counts absences, shares or rating lost will rank them
together; only a measure that asks *what the shedding got him* separates them.

## Re-hearing of the 3 players the tribunal convicted

All 3 bans upheld. This time the review had fields the tribunal never saw: when the competition
started, the rating and the clock time at registration. It also used `Stats.dbo.GameSessions`,
which no earlier cycle had looked at.

**4 possible tests were tried on all 16 reviewed players. All 4 failed.** Each one fires on
acquitted players as often as on convicted ones:

- how far ahead he registers before the start - the widest gap belongs to `M4M Ovi`, acquitted
- how much better he places below a bracket line than above it - widest for `JoeCoviDodo` and
  `flacheman2`, both acquitted. Anyone who plays both sides of a line shows this. It says the
  bracket below is weaker, not that the player put himself there
- whether he stops taking an hour he never attends - nobody stops. Every player in the group keeps
  taking his worst hour just as often in the 2nd half of the period as in the 1st
- whether he sits in the game through a competition he registered for and skipped - highest for
  `xFenrir77`, the plainest ordinary player in the group, who did it for the full 2 hours 9 times

**1 test survived.** Count only what the player himself already knew when he registered: an hour
where he had 3 or more finished entries and had turned up to none of them. `HavocHHH` and
`LEK_TARNO` rank 1st and 2nd at every threshold from 1 to 4 prior misses. `xFenrir77`, `kojldyn`,
`flacheman2` and `JoeCoviDodo` score 0 at every threshold. Being genuinely absent is not a defence
here. It is what makes the slot a safe place to drop rating.

**The offence, counted, sets a floor but does not convict.** Suppression and prizes-taken-below
also catch `MP_Alan`, `kojldyn`, `JoeCoviDodo` and `flacheman2`, all acquitted on their
trajectories. It does clear cleanly in the other direction: the 3 players who never take a prize
across a bracket line score 0 on it.

- **LEK_TARNO** - upheld. He registered 20 times for hours his own record already showed he never
  attends. That cost him 230 rating. On 09-20, at rating 206, he took the 20:00 and 22:00 slots. He
  had missed those 2 hours 8 and 7 times running and turned up to none of them. Session records
  show he really is away in the evening. That is what makes those slots reliable, not what excuses
  them. Suppression 506, and 9 of his 12 prizes came below the level he had earned - the highest in
  the group on both counts.
- **Gentleman83190** - upheld. He registered 49 times and entered 10. On 09-13, just after placing
  7th in MIDDLES, he skipped 6 competitions in a row while logged in for most of each one. At the
  level he had earned he places 10th and 7th. At the bottom he places 1st, 3rd, 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 1st.
- **Rapidstrapon** - upheld. He registered 61 times and entered 18. On 09-18 he took all 12 slots
  of the day, missed 11 in a row while his rating fell from 109 to 0, then entered the 12th at 0
  and came 2nd. He was in the game for 22 of his 43 absences.

**`tournamentLog` cannot say whether a player was online.** For `LEK_TARNO`, 620 of his 622
anti-cheat lines fall inside competitions he entered. The log therefore can never show a player
sitting in the game while a competition he registered for runs without him. `GameSessions` is
written all the time and is not cleaned, so it covers the whole period. Query kept in
`game-sessions-2026-09-20.sql`.

**Entrance cost is not a signal and should be dropped from the reasoning.** Entry is paid in
non-premium currency, which players hold in the millions after level 30. The cost does track what
is at stake - it lines up with the no-show penalty at -0.92 and with the points for 1st place at
+0.87, and a zero-score costs exactly half a no-show - but the penalty stops growing at -20 once
the entry passes 3000. More to the point, the player has no choice: across the whole period none
of the 16 ever entered 2 competitions starting at the same time, so each slot offers his bracket
exactly 1 competition. The only way to pick a harder one is to wait for it, which costs a drainer
the thing he wants least. Measured, none of them do: the convicted sit at or below the group
average of -14.2 rating per absence.

## Findings

**The detector counts crossings; the competent abuser caps.** Five cases across two cycles now:
`CrazyGepard` at exactly 100, `Bas_di08` at exactly 1000, `HavocHHH` at 988, `MP_Alan` at 987,
`flacheman2` at 75. 100 is the highest rating that is still NOOBS and 1000 the highest still
MIDDLES. Every counter reads zero on a player who stops there. Recorded in the backlog together
with the formal statement of the offence that supersedes the rule set.

**The log is missing 85 participations of 961 across the 15 rebuilt cards — nearly 9%.** Not only
zero-delta rows: 48 carry a zero delta but 36 carry a real one, including **32 no-shows with
penalties up to -20**. Every trajectory read this year has been missing part of the shedding. All
15 cards nevertheless reported `evidence_completeness: ok`, so step 4.5 does not catch it. Where
the log does carry a reward line its delta agreed with SQL on every overlapping row — the log does
not lie, it omits.

**The card span has the same boundary defect the sweep window had.** Six competitions starting
2026-09-06, one slot before the card's 09-07 bound, had their rewards applied on 09-07 and so
appeared in the log-built ledger while being absent from the SQL spine.

**The weekly Rating board is not a route for this vector** and needs no recurring check: it pays
for rating *gained* and the offence consists of shedding it. Measured — best cohort placings are
48th on Steam, 100th on PS. The single hit, `xFenrir77` 7th on Xbox, is there precisely because he
is not a drainer. Monthly and yearly **Won** remain live concerns; they accumulate across weeks
while a 4-week ban does not cover a month.

## Method

What worked this cycle was not the tribunal. The three deadline convictions it produced were
reproducible from a handful of columns, and it acquitted at confidence 9 the two players the
operator identified by eye in under a minute. What produced every subsequent decision was the
trajectory read directly, with the competing explanations tested one against another.

The card's ledger was rebuilt mid-cycle to carry the fields that made that possible:
competition **start** time (not only the moment the rating was applied), rating at registration,
rating carried into the bout, place, entrance fee, and the registration timestamp from the log.
`Registered` is what exposes batch registration; `Applied` doubles as evidence of when the player
was actually in the game.
